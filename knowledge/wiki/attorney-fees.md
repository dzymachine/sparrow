# Requesting Attorney's Fees and Costs Based on Need

## Plain English
If your spouse earns much more than you, they may be able to afford a lawyer while you cannot. California law allows you to ask the judge to order your spouse to pay some or all of your legal fees so that both sides have fair access to legal representation.

## Statutory Rules & Requirements
- **Family Code Section 2030:** If the court finds a disparity in access to funds to retain counsel and that one party has the ability to pay for both sides, the court **shall** order attorney's fees and costs [S5].
- **Required Proof (Form FL-319):** Form FL-319 requires demonstrating that the other party has or is reasonably likely to have the ability to pay for both parties [S4].
- **Form Package (Rule of Court 5.427):**
  1. Form FL-300 (Request for Order)
  2. Form FL-319 (Request for Attorney's Fees and Costs Attachment)
  3. Form FL-150 (Current Income and Expense Declaration)
  4. Form FL-158 (Supporting Declaration) or comparable written statement [S3].
- **Procedural Strictness:** In *Sharples v. Sharples* (2014) 223 Cal. App. 4th 160, a fee request was denied because Form FL-319 was omitted from the court filing [S13].
