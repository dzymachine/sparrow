# Sharing the Money Picture (Financial Disclosures)

## Plain English
Both you and your spouse have to show each other your income, what you own, and what you owe. This is required by California law (Fam. Code §§ 2102, 2104, 2105), and it is one of the places the law works in your favor: your spouse has to disclose too.

## What It Involves
- **FL-140** (cover form), **FL-142** (assets and debts), and **FL-150** (income and expenses) are served on your spouse. FL-140 and FL-142 are confidential and not filed with the court [S7].
- **FL-150** requires proof of income for the past two months attached (e.g. pay stubs, W-2s) [S8].
- **FL-141** is the declaration filed with the court to verify disclosures were served [S7].
- **Timing:** Mandatory at filing or within 60 days [S6]. The 60-day window can be changed by written agreement or court order [S21].

## Document Discovery & Uncovered Accounts
If a spouse was kept out of the household accounts, she cannot list what she doesn't know exists. Uploading financial documents (tax returns, W-2s, mortgage statements) allows detection of possible account gaps—such as Schedule B interest/dividend lines or 401(k) deductions without matching statements—to add to the lawyer brief [S7, S8].

## Agent & User Guidance
- Phrase gaps as questions to ask a lawyer or request in formal discovery, never as criminal accusations.
- Always log identified document gaps in the Lawyer Prep Brief.
